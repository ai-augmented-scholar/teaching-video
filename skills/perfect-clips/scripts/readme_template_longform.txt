PERFECT CLIPS — what's in this folder (WIDE CLIPS)
==================================================

These clips come from one of your lecture videos. The perfect-clips skill
found the complete explanations inside it, cut them frame by frame, removed
the dead weight from inside each one, and burned in one-word captions along
the bottom. The picture is untouched: same resolution, same shape, same
framing as the original. Only the cuts changed.

Use them on a course page, where a student watches one complete explanation
in a wide player. They are plain MP4 files: any video editor (iMovie, or any
other) and any course site or learning management system accepts them.

THE CLIPS
  The numbered MP4s are ranked best first. The number after "WIDE" is the
  score out of 100 (hook, engagement, value and shareability, 25 each). Each
  one is a whole segment, an explanation or an argument, with a beginning, a
  middle and a payoff.

  Wide clips carry no on-screen headline and no music bed, and the caption
  line stays low and still, clear of a web player's control bar.

  Fewer clips than you expected is a real answer. An hour of lecture usually
  holds three or four complete explanations. Shorter moments suit upright
  clips; the REVIVE sheet says which.

TITLES + DESCRIPTIONS.txt
  A short title and a one- or two-sentence description for every clip.

REVIVE - candidates.csv
  Every candidate the skill considered, kept and skipped, with scores and
  reasons. The "mode" column says whether a row was cut as an upright clip
  or a wide one. To get a skipped one, ask Claude to "revive" it by number.

clip data/
  The machine files: per-clip cut data (JSON), caption word timings, SRT
  captions, and a Premiere/Resolve XML per clip. The wide clips also get a
  clean SRT caption file beside them, ready for a course site that takes a
  caption upload.

The MP4s stand on their own. The XML files point at the ORIGINAL video:
if that file moves or gets a new name, re-link it in your editor.
