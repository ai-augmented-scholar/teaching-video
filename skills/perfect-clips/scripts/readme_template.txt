PERFECT CLIPS — what's in this folder (UPRIGHT CLIPS)
=====================================================

These short clips come from one of your lecture videos. The perfect-clips
skill found the moments that stand on their own, cut them frame by frame,
turned them upright for phone screens, and burned in one-word captions plus
a short headline over the first seconds of each clip.

Use them for course announcements, the course homepage, or a teaser for
next week's video. They are plain MP4 files: any video editor (iMovie, or
any other) and any course site or learning management system accepts them.

THE CLIPS
  The numbered MP4s are ranked best first. The number after "CLIP" is the
  score out of 100 (hook, engagement, value and shareability, 25 each).
  Watch from the top down and keep the ones you like.

  Some clips open with a TEASER: a line from inside the clip plays first,
  then the whole moment plays through, so you hear that line again where it
  belongs. This is on purpose: the viewer keeps watching to see it arrive.

TITLES + DESCRIPTIONS.txt
  A short title and a one- or two-sentence description for every clip, plus
  the headline each clip carries on screen.

REVIVE - candidates.csv
  Every candidate the skill considered, kept and skipped, with scores and
  reasons. To get a skipped one, ask Claude to "revive" it by number; it
  renders without transcribing the video again.

clip data/
  The machine files: per-clip cut data (JSON), caption word timings, SRT
  captions, and a Premiere/Resolve XML per clip. Editors that read XML can
  re-open any clip on the original footage with the cuts already in place.

A clip may carry a quiet music bed under the voice, if you set a music
folder. The voice always stays on top.

The MP4s stand on their own. The XML files point at the ORIGINAL video:
if that file moves or gets a new name, re-link it in your editor.
